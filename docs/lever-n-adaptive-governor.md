# The adaptive Lever N governor (QWEN_FAST_LEVERN_ADAPTIVE)

Status: built and unit-tested on the CPU, never run on a card. Gate only, default off, and byte for byte today's behaviour when off. Every number in the
"Expected effect" section is an ESTIMATE from the repository's timing model, not a measurement.

## What it changes and why

Today the Lever N alternator paces a long prefill against the live decoders at a prefill share `f = QWEN_FAST_LEVERN_PREFILL_SHARE` (0.5): after a prefill step of
`dt` ms it owes the decoders `dt x (1 - f) / f` ms of decode rounds (capped at KMAX rounds), at least one round. The deadline governor
(`levern_policy.effective_share`) raises `f` for a long prompt just far enough to finish within `QWEN_FAST_LEVERN_TTFT_TARGET_S` (180 s). That is the right
trade with seven decoders live; with one or two it gives the long prompt half the wall time for the sake of two users, and a cold 254k prompt rides to its 180 s
target although the device needs about 98 s for it.

The adaptive policy moves the governor's two inputs while FEW decoders are live, and nothing else:

| input to the governor | decoders live: 0 | 1 to `ADAPTIVE_DECODERS` (2) | more than that |
|---|---|---|---|
| base prefill share | today's (nothing is owed with no decoder) | `max(PREFILL_SHARE, ADAPTIVE_SHARE)` = 0.9 | today's |
| deadline target | today's | `min(TTFT_TARGET_S, ADAPTIVE_TTFT_S)` = `min(180, 120)` | today's |

The count is the live decoders at the scheduling decision (the scheduler's own `running` minus its partial prefills), re-read at every step: a third decoder
arriving returns the very next step to today's pacing, and the decoders leaving return it to the adaptive one. The governor's output is still clamped to
`[base, 1.0]`; `Alternator.govern` computes `base` and `target` and then runs the same `effective_share` and `governor_need` calls it ran before.

Not changed: the admission rule (one fresh prefill per step, the short lane, parking, the KV reservation and the DRAM hold), the step sizes (2,048 tokens while
a decoder runs or another prompt waits, 16,384 alone), the chunk boundaries and the engine build, so every prefill step is the step it was and the text is
unchanged; only WHEN the decode rounds run between the steps moves.

## The flags (gate only)

| flag | default | meaning |
|---|---|---|
| `QWEN_FAST_LEVERN_ADAPTIVE` | 0 | `1` turns the policy on; unset or 0 runs, logs and decides exactly as before |
| `QWEN_FAST_LEVERN_ADAPTIVE_DECODERS` | 2 | the most live decoders that still count as few, 1 to 7 |
| `QWEN_FAST_LEVERN_ADAPTIVE_SHARE` | 0.9 | the base share, a decimal in (0, 1); 1.0 is refused (the static control arm's stall) |
| `QWEN_FAST_LEVERN_ADAPTIVE_TTFT_S` | 120 | the lowered target in whole seconds; 0 leaves the profile's target |

Strict like every Lever N flag: a malformed value, a knob without the switch, the switch without `QWEN_FAST_LEVER_N=1` and the merged route
(`QWEN_PREFIX_REUSE=1`, where the deadline governor and the floor live), static rounds (`QWEN_FAST_LEVERN_ROUNDS` overrides the share) and a share of 1.0 are
configuration errors (`levern_policy.adaptive_problems`, refused at the profile contract and at the scheduler install by name). The profile contract refuses
all four on a traffic profile. The gate-only twin of the production profile is `c2-packed-tp4-8x262k-ship-prefix-levern-w2-er-adaptive`, generated by
`scripts/ci/make_adaptive_profiles.py` (production plus exactly the four names above, no waiver).

## The decode-gap floor is untouched

`QWEN_FAST_LEVERN_MAX_DECODE_GAP_S` (8 s) guarantees the decoders a round at least every G seconds while the governor holds the prefill at all the device.
Nothing in the floor changed, and the policy REQUIRES it (a floor of 0 is refused):

- below a governed 1.0 the alternator owes a decode round after every prefill step (`RMIN`), so the decoders wait at most one prefill step (about 0.4 to 1.2 s);
- at a governed 1.0 (the adaptive target of 120 s cannot hold two queued 254k prompts, as 180 s could not) `owes()` yields when `gap_exceeded()`, every G
  seconds, and a short prefill is still paced at one round.

Held by tests: `test_levern_policy.AdaptiveFloorTests` re-runs every floor test with the policy in force; `test_levern_prefix_scheduler.AdaptiveGovernorTests`
replays the scheduler, the two-cold-prompt shape and the whole concurrent8_skew shape and runs the smoke check's alternation and floor rules over the scheduler's
own step lines, and 200 seeded random schedules assert that two prefill steps in a row with decoders waiting only happen at a governed 1.0 inside the floor, and
that the adaptive share is never below the control's for the same input (both with negative controls: disabling the floor or lowering the share fails them).

## What is logged

With the policy off, no line differs (a test compares the whole log of two schedules). With it on: one install line,

    [PINDIAG] lever N adaptive: decoders<=2 share=0.9 ttft=120 gap_floor=8

and every step line gains three fields after `f_eff= need= gap_ms=`: `base=` (the share the governor started from at that step), `target=` (the deadline target
it used, whole seconds, 0 off) and `adaptive=` (1 while the policy was in force). `c2_smoke_check.levern_adaptive_problems` reads them: no trace at all with the
flag off, the install line and the fields with it on, every prefill step with 1 to N decoders at `adaptive=1` from `max(share, 0.9)` and the lowered target, every
other prefill step at `adaptive=0` from today's share and target, `f_eff` never below `base`, and `prefill_few_decoders` NOT EXERCISED when it ran and no step
was adaptive.

## Expected effect (ESTIMATED)

Time to first token of a cold long prompt with one or two decoders live, from the timing model of `levern_policy.StepTimes` (a 2,048-token chunk costs
`420 ms + 2.5 ms per 1,000 tokens of context`, 40 ms a step, 2.5 s for the engine; the seeds are NOT measured at 254k) and decode rounds of 100 to 170 ms:

| prompt | today (share 0.5, deadline-governed) | adaptive (0.9) | change |
|---|---|---|---|
| 32,768 | 18.7 to 19.0 s | 12.0 to 13.0 s | -31 to -37 % |
| 131,072 | 84 to 87 s | 49 to 53 s | -37 to -44 % |
| 253,920 | 182 to 187 s (rides to the 180 s target) | 114 to 119 s | -35 to -39 % |

The device needs 98.5 s for the 254k prompt alone, so about 15 to 20 s of the adaptive figure is the decoders' whole share and the one-round minimum. Shorts
(at most `QWEN_FAST_LEVERN_SHORT_TOKENS`, 16,384, left to compute) are paced at one round whatever the share and do not move. The cost is the decoders': while a
long prompt prefills, one or two users get about a tenth of the wall time in decode rounds instead of half, so their inter-token rate during that window falls
about five-fold for a window about 40 % shorter. Their worst gap stays one prefill step (and the floor) either way. Whether that is a good trade is the owner's
decision; the policy ships off and the production profile does not name it.

With three or more decoders live (the production load) the pacing is today's, step for step; the card A/B below checks it.

## Card jobs (references/tp4-prefill-jobs, never run)

ABAB of the production profile (A) against the twin (B) on one image, timed with the CI runners paused: `concurrent8_skew` (two cold 254k prompts beside six shorts, eight
live: today's pacing must be unchanged, and the slowest first token inside the 238 s limit), `concurrent8_code_32k` (eight 32k users, the same) and
`prefill_few_decoders` (one decoder with a 120k cold prompt, then two with a 254k one: the policy's own shape). Read A against B with
`scripts/ci/prefill_ladder_report.py A.json --second B.json`: the few-decoder TTFT ratio B over A (the estimate says 0.6 to 0.65), the decoders' longest gaps and window
progress, the cold arrivals' answer hashes equal between the arms (greedy text byte-identical), and the skew and 32k windows within the A-to-A drift with
`scripts/ci/w2ln_timing_compare.py`. NO-GO: any text difference, a floor violation (`levern_alternation_problems`), a skew first token over the limit, or an eight-live
window outside the drift.
